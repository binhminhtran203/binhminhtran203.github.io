About me

I am a final-year Bachelor of Advanced Computing (Honours) student in Computational Data Science at the University of Sydney. My research lies at the intersection of biomedical artificial intelligence, graph representation learning, multimodal learning, spatial omics, and computational oncology.

My Honours research focuses on developing robust and interpretable machine-learning methods for breast cancer survival prediction by integrating spatial single-cell imaging, clinicopathologic variables, and gene-level data. In particular, I am developing HiNT-Surv, a hierarchical niche-aware graph-learning framework designed to capture cellular organisation within the tumour microenvironment while supporting cross-cohort and cross-panel generalisation.

Beyond my Honours project, I have contributed to research involving multimodal breast cancer receptor prediction, biological graph learning, medical image analysis, and clinical risk modelling. My work has resulted in peer-reviewed publications and manuscripts across biomedical AI and graph machine learning. Looking ahead, I am interested in research focused on multimodal biomedical AI, spatial omics, graph neural networks, and clinically reliable machine learning.

Experience

Feb 2026
– Present

Honours Research Student

School of Computer Science, The University of Sydney · Supervisor: A/Prof Xiuying Wang

▸DetailsHide details
  • Designed HiNT-Surv, a hierarchical niche-aware model that fuses imaging mass cytometry (IMC) with clinicopathologic and gene-level variables for survival prediction; evaluated on 404 METABRIC and 285 Basel patients (834 tissue cores, 50- and 52-marker panels).
  • Parameterised the encoder by antigen identity rather than fixed channel order, so a model trained on one antibody panel transfers to another; kept all preprocessing and label-free pretraining inside each outer fold's training partition to prevent patient-level leakage.
  • Reached Harrell's C-index 0.740 ± 0.020 (METABRIC) and 0.734 ± 0.030 (Basel) under patient-level cross-validation — gains of 0.028 and 0.057 over the clinical Cox-PH baseline — with median-risk stratification separating Kaplan–Meier curves in both cohorts (two-sided log-rank p < 10⁻⁴).
  • Trained on METABRIC and evaluated on Basel without target-cohort fine-tuning, reaching C-index 0.697 against 0.631 for the strongest published comparator, with a smaller source-to-target decrease (0.043 vs 0.081).
